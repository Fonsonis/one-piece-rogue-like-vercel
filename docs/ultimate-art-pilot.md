# Ultimate sprite pilot: Zoro

The original `public/art/characters/zoro.png` remains the normal attack. The
new three-sword sequence is `public/art/characters/ultimates/zoro.png`. It was
generated by the built-in `image_gen` tool from the original atlas, then packed
with `scripts/import-ultimate-poses.py`. Its full-resolution source is
`docs/ultimate-art-sources/zoro.png`.

## Generation prompts

First edit, with `public/art/characters/zoro.png` as the image reference:

> Use case: precise-object-edit. Asset type: new pixel-art four-frame ULTIMATE combat sprite sheet for Zoro. Reference image is the current normal attack sheet; preserve his exact identity: short green hair, white shirt, green haramaki belt, black trousers, three sword hilts, proportions and crisp pixel-art rendering. Produce a NEW physical three-sword technique, not the reference single blue slash: frame 1 low three-sword guard with one katana clenched in his mouth and two drawn in his hands; frame 2 crouched cross-body windup with both arm swords pulled behind; frame 3 decisive full-body forward spinning three-sword cross cut, torso and legs in a dramatically different silhouette, three blades clearly visible; frame 4 recovery with all three blades and bent knees. Arrange four isolated full-body characters in equal-width horizontal panels with clear empty gutters, same baseline and consistent character size. Transparent alpha background. Each pose should fit entirely inside its panel including sword tips. The action must be readable at small combat-card size. No colored aura, no outline glow, no mere recolor, no existing pose reused, no giant special-effects shapes, no words, labels, borders or watermark.

Second edit, with the first generated result as the reference:

> Use case: precise-object-edit. Refine this four-pose Zoro pixel-art ultimate sprite sheet. Keep all four genuinely different three-sword body poses, costume, face, green hair, shirt, sash and swords. Critical layout repair: set exactly four nonoverlapping equal-width horizontal cells, each character INCLUDING every sword tip entirely inside its own cell. Add at least 45 pixels of completely transparent gutter around every cell boundary, especially between poses 3 and 4. Scale down a little if needed so no blade touches or crosses a cell boundary. Align feet to one baseline. Remove any bright red/yellow edge fringing; preserve clean original-style pixel edges and real alpha transparency. No aura, colored glow, background, panels, text or borders.

## Import

The generated sheet was 2170×725 RGBA. Although the pose bounds overlap in
horizontal position, each character is a separate alpha-connected component.
The importer identifies the four main bodies by alpha, assigns any detached
weapons or fists to the nearest body, and packs them into four 192×192 cells.
It copies the first cell directly from the original attack atlas so the idle
pose is identical in both versions. It fails if four main bodies cannot be
identified safely.

```sh
python3 scripts/import-ultimate-poses.py zoro docs/ultimate-art-sources/zoro.png
```

The packed atlas was visually reviewed at its native 768×192 size. The first
pose is pixel-identical to Zoro's normal attack atlas. The three animation
poses retain his costume and swords, have transparent backgrounds, and stay
inside their cells. The impact silhouette differs from his normal attack.
